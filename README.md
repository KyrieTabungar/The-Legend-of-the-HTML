        <h1><center>The Legend of the HTML</center></h1>
        <h4><center>By Kyrie Tabungar</center></h4>
        <p>This story is on the very tips of your fingers, the organized numbers and letters making up mazes of codes and conduct, all lined in harmonious order to create the land this very tale takes place in; the kingdom of Codexus. The residents of the kingdom are as organized as the inside of a CPU, each have their own respective jobs, beliefs, and work stations. They were so organized, in fact, it was almost depressing, there was order, sure, but the order left no room for creativity nor creation. But one Symbol sought different, with a heart of gold and a mind of pure imagination, dreamt of a world where both creativity and order would prosper. Hatmul, a young mind with great, great aspirations, he was a cheery little fellow, always there when someone needed help, mostly because he didn't belong anywhere, all he does all day in the kingdom is look for random work oppurtunities and watch the other Codexians work their miserable jobs. One day, Hatmul decides to do work inside King Codex's castle, both for the job and to look around. He looks up at the king a lot, for he's the one who founded Codexus, bringing in all kinds of Symbolians from the great Rectangle and uniting them as one kingdom, Hatmul is just as reverent as all the other Codexians about him, despite the mass of order and grayness their king forced upon them.</p>
        <p>Once Hatmul enters the castle gates, he is astonished by the neon streaks and the buzzing glow of code running through the very walls of the palace, the sight catches him off guard, spinning around more than once to really gauge his surroundings, before he feels his back press against hard metal. Hatmul tilts his head up and immediately recognizes the big, white, bushy beard and glowing armor made of pure code—King Codex himself. Hatmul scrambles to fix himself up and stand in front of the king, smiling in both embarrassment and awe. "It's...such an honor to meet you face to face, King Codex, your highness, because- we only ever see you on the screens of Codexus regarding announcements." Hatmul continues to bare his teeth behind his twitching smile at the king, his mouth clearly running faster than his brain. The king just laughs, amused by both his enthusiasm and nervousness. "No worries, son, your reaction to my presence is none of abnormality. Please, Hatmul, make yourself at home." King Codex's words of hospitality fill Hatmul's chest with relief that he doesn't have to put up a facade in front of the king. "The job we currently have available is cleaning work in the dungeon, that shouldn't be of any difficulty for you, will it?" King Codex's smile is lined with his full, thick beard, it's contagious, Hatmul couldn't help but crack a grin himself. "No, of course not, sire! The dungeon should be in tip-top shape in-" Hatmul's words come to a halt as soon as he realizes the meaning of them. "...Hold on, I'm sorry—Dungeon?" He asks out of turn, almost coming off as invasive thanks to how quick he was to question. The king's smile dims ever so slightly. "Yes, dungeon. No worries, it isn't in use, it's only there just in case a Codexian steps out of line." King Codex explains calmly, as if it's completely normal to have a dungeon for a strictly organized kingdom, and it is. Hatmul's vast mind doesn't buy it for a moment, but it sinks in anyway, his smile returning. "Alright, your highness, I understand. In that case, I'll get to work immediately." Hatmul doesn't hesitate to start off his shift, wanting time alone to think and at the same time finish the day. The king watches him head down to the darker areas of the palace to enter the neon-glow-lit dungeon—he doesn't seem to be amused by his awareness. Despite Hatmul's firsthand suspicion of King Codex, he doesn't hesitate to start cleanup on the dungeon, starting with the floors. He grabs a bucket and a mop from a nearby supply closet and gets to work on the stained floors, sweeping and wiping off all sorts of muck on the stone-plaid surface. Hatmul was in a cleaning frenzy down there, fully focused on cleaning every point and pebble in the spacious room. But as quickly as he got to work—he was snapped out of his trance by a faint rustling coming from one of the cells, he stiffens in his spot, eyeing the darkness engulfing the corner of the room where the noise came from—"...Please..." A low, weak, pitiful voice echoes from out of cell and into Hatmul's ears, he drops his mop, the wooden neck slamming softly on the stone floor, it seems to startle whatever was behind the metal bars. "I'm sorry- I'm sorry... I was a little scared too, I thought no one would be in here." Hatmul raises his hands in front of him, trying to look as non-hostile as possible as to not frighten the individual, or whatever was in there. "...May I know who you are?" He continues to conversate, hoping to communicate. "...Codex."</p>
        <p>Hatmul visibly flinches—Codex? Does King Codex have a twin brother? Is he a copy and paste? All questions die out as one pins into his mind: "Why are you in the dungeon?" He asks, concern all over his face as he tries to make out the other person through the darkness. Codex steps into the soft glow of the code around the walls only outside the jail cells—he looks exactly like the king, just more rugged and chipped around the edges—from his appearance alone, Hatmul could tell that he'd spent a long time in here. "Malwar, he banished me here after the last of the Languages had been erased." Codex grips the bars as he looks at Hatmul with tired, aged eyes. Hatmul gasps softly, he looks identical to king Codex, beard, mighty build, stoic figure, how is this possible? And—Malwar? Devourer of codes? Wasn't he defeated eons ago by Codex himself? So why is he down here rotting away in a dungeon, while another one is up there ruling an entire kingdom? "How do I set you free? Are you in chains?" Hatmul immediately comes to Codex's aid, trying to find a way to get him out of the cell, checking for any locks or handles. "No, stop! I have to stay in hiding or else Malwar may do something to me, or worse, to you." Codex warns Hatmul, but it's already too late. "I'm sure he may." King Codex's booming, authoritative tone echoes throughout the dungeon halls, standing by the stairs and staring into both Hatmul and Codex's eyes in intimidating horror. "Codex, I'm oh so disappointed that you decided to abandon your protocol, especially after all these eons of being such an obedient prisoner." King Codex shakes his head in dissatisfaction, his tone almost mocking. "Your highness, set him free, because I'd like some answers." Hatmul stands his ground, wanting the truth out of both of them. "You'll get your answers, Hatmul, but they'll all be from me." King Codex flicks his wrist, in a split second, code starts to dissipate from Codex's veins, destroying the jailed man from the inside. "AAAGGGGHH- NO!" Codex falls to his knees, code flickers and fades from his body as his life starts to leave him slowly. "NO! CODEX!" Hatmul panics as he watches Codex spasm and get weaker and weaker from King Codex's abilities. "You see, Hatmul, I am Malwar," Malwar explains his true identity as he continues to sadistically drain the life force out of Codex. "I took this useless fool's position as king and lead Codexus, how kind of me, right?" Hatmul doesn't fall for it, someone this evil needs to have an underlying plan. "No! You're the code devourer! You're only ruling the kingdom because...-" Hatmul pales as he pieces together Malwar's true intentions for the kingdom of Codexus: He's going to devour everything as soon as Codexus is large enough. "...Y-You're making everyone work so that the code grows, so that you'll eat everything after!" Hatmul is furious from this revelation, Malwar was secretly taking control of everyone for his own gain, and he thought it was just to keep the peace! "Correct, you learn fast, son." Malwar grins from ear to ear maliciously, Hatmul just stands there, wide-eyed as he watches Codex fade, half his body crumbling into nothing but bits of numbers and codes, merging into the thousands of layers of other different codes in the walls of the castle. "Hatmul..." Codex wheezes, his body almost disappearing completely. "You are...an HT-..." gone, bid to nothing but code for the palace. Hatmul couldn't even mourn, he met him so fast, and then gone just as quick. "Now that you know the truth, Hatmul, you can either betray me and end up like your 'mighty' friend, or be my apprentice and I'll spare you at the devouring." Malwar leaves Hatmul with the pain-staking choices, climbing back up the stairs and into his chambers.</p>
        <p>The next few days were a blur, Hatmul's mind never left the night when the real Codex disintegrated into the walls, destroyed by his nemesis, Malwar, who disguised himself as him to manipulate the kingdom of Codexus to working to enlarge the code, the running heart of the kingdom, all for him to feast on. That catastrophe will happen really soon if Hatmul doesn't do something about it. He continues his work in the castle, he doesn't exactly have a choice since Malwar basically has him hostage. Today, Hatmul is found reorganizing books in the library since new ones came in, sorting each book in their respective shelves in every aisle, careful with each page and cover as he slides them between each other. Just as Hatmul was sorting the last of the books, he came across an odd one—old, a little torn, but the pages could still be flipped, so out of instinct, he sits down on a nearby table and decides to take a peek. —Sanctuary of the Symbolians— Hatmul's interest was already piqued once he reads the cover, he flips the page to the table of contents: Bodies, Heads, Titles, Paragraphs—it was a book about the different types of code Symbols, he continues to read, his eyes scanning every line of information for every Symbol, how Symbols work, how they provide, how they're created, why they're created... Hatmul can't stop reading, he didn't know the history of the Symbol residents went this deep, but that makes him wonder; where does he belong? He continues to flip the pages, searching for anything that could connect to what his purpose is. After flipping a few fifty pages, he catches something, something familiar—HTML. HTML, what could that mean? He knows Codex mentioned it once while telling him about Malwar, that Malwar erased all of the Coding Languages, but why? Hatmul keeps flipping, and flipping, and flipping—until he comes across a torn part of the book. "HTML Symbol Purpose and Abilities: Crea-" Hatmul reads the only information seen on the ripped page, it was the most crucial one, he sighs in frustration before closing the book. All the reading about the HTML's remind Hatmul of something, the last thing Codex said to him before fading into the palace—"You are an HT-"—did he mean to tell him that he is one of the HTML? But that's impossible, he's never felt any sort of magical surge within him nor have any strong sense of responsibility, he does check off the creative and imaginative trait though, but that isn't enough to convince him. Hatmul goes up to Malwar's chamber to make an excuse to enter, he needs to look for that ripped page. He makes his way through the red carpet-lined hallways, his steps hesitant but purposeful, he eyes the large wooden door ahead of him and stops right in front of it, before knocking firmly. "...King Codex? Sire?" The fake name on his tongue stings him slightly, Hatmul tries to sound as confident as possible, and he's succeeding, mostly because it's a door he's in front of and not the Code Devourer Malwar. But the terror himself does answer, wearing the disguise of an innocent Symbol, and a warm smile, hiding a thousand burning seas of greed under that grin. "Hatmul, good day, what do you need?" Hatmul swallows his fear and answers with all the genuineness he can muster: "I'd like to offer to tidy up your chambers-" Malwar cuts him off immediately. "You found the book, and you're searching for the missing page. Yes, I have it." He says in the most unamused tone Hatmul has ever heard from anyone, it scared him that he already knew about his antics, but he was even more dumbfounded when Malwar leans in to speak again, this time, with a wider grin and lower volume, the rumble of his voice ringing in Hatmul's ears: "Do what you want with it, it doesn't matter. The feast has already begun."</p>
        <p>Hatmul stiffens, eyes going blank as he just stares at Malwar in disbelief, he ceases to believe him for a moment, shaking his head, before near sprinting to the nearest window to look at the state of Codexus, silently hoping he was bluffing and that the kingdom would be fine—he wasn't. Hatmul looks down at a decaying city, families holding their children close as they disappear from their arms, buildings toppling over themselves as the walls dissolve into code, Symbolians running for their lives but ending up as piles of numbers anyway. Hatmul couldn't bear to see the suffering, his eyes glossy with a furious fervor fueling the now raging fire within him, his head snaps back at Malwar, expecting him to be laughing in his face by now, but he's disappeared, the doorway to his chambers wide open. "Kingdom of Codexus! Plead your final prayers, for Lord Malwar shall enjoy your pleas!" Malwar's bellowing voice boomed across the streets of Codexus, terror filling the hearts of every resident as they recognize the voice they dreaded to hear once more after the eons of peace and tranquility that prospered in their kingdom. The King Codex disguise is abandoned, finally revealing his true form to all of the Kingdom of Codexus, his wide, sharp-toothed grin being showcased all over the screens as his ominous purple aura spreads through the now panicked and chaotic neighborhoods of the kingdom. His dark indigo robes flow behind his tall lanky frame, exposing the true nature of his greed and gluttony for codes like the virus he is. Hatmul doesn't hesitate to sprint into the chambers to search for the missing page, he checks under the bed, the mattress, every closet, every crack in the floor, in the wall, in between the books on the shelves, he finds nothing. He was just about to give up right there, but he finds his eyes raking over the brick wall in the corner, curious, he gets up and checks it out, running his fingers over every brick slowly, hoping to find one that—sticks out. He felt it, there was something odd about this one brick, it moves a little when he presses on it, instinctively, he pushes it completely—the brick falls out of the wall and onto the stone floor, leaving a gaping whole where it was before. Hatmul peeks into the hole, trying to gauge the inside despite the darkness, and there he sees it, a sheet of paper, he grabs it immediately and unrolls the dusty page, blowing some of the dust off it, before reading it's contents. "-tion, Structurization, and Organization. Higher Symbol's may have the ability to create new Symbolians and code sequences, but all is without use in the lack of a Language to define and stabilize each one. HTML isn't as powerful as JavaScript or Python, but their presence alone is enough to beacon other Languages with their Websitorial Display abilities." Hatmul breath hitches once he learns that he can signal other Languages, other Languages that can potentially aid them in their ongoing catastrophe, Languages from beyond the Rectangle. He continues to read, hoping to find a way to create a beacon, and fast. "HTML's use basic Symbolians as tools to harness their Websitorial Display, such as Bodies, Heads, and Paragraphs." Hatmul is met with a dead end, how can he use the Symbolians if they're all being slaughtered by Lord Malwar? He stops to think, looking around, at the books, the tapestry, the carpet, then his eyes zero in on the walls, the sequence of numbers and letters flowing through each small transparent pipe, creating an conspicuous, pulsing blue glow that caught Hatmul's eye. It clicks in his mind, every Symbolian is made of pure code, so he could try and recreate mock Symbols using the left over code in the walls so he can unlock his Websitorial Creation and Display ability. He doesn't hesitate, taking a few steps back and bracing himself for impact, before ramming into the wall with all the force he could muster, his shoulder slams against the metal wall, creating a small dent, but not enough to break the pipes inside, he tries again, ramming into the wall with the same strength, nothing yet, he does it one last time, he sprints at full speed and elbows the dented metal wall as hard as he can—CRASH! The glass pipes inside are smashed as liquid code bursts and oozes out of the wall, jumbled up numbers and letters spill onto the floor below Hatmul. He exhales heavily, relieved that it worked, he slowly reaches to touch the blue code, his fingers sinking into the liquid for a moment, before it rises—circling around him like the Earth would to the Sun. Hatmul shuts his eyes closed when he feels an unfamiliar warmth churning within him, it almost felt like—power. The liquid code revolving around him slowly changes it's hue once it's conscious of it's user, from blue—to a neon orange. Hatmul notices this change, clearly knowing what this meant: he's unlocked his abilities. He tests it out, waving his hands around to control the direction of the code, and it follows him, trailing his fingers as it floats mid-air, he's fascinated by how the liquid moves with him, as if it was part of his very being, it most likely is. He decides to see if he can start using the code to create the mock Symbols and make a Websitorial Beacon, focusing all his energy into making a Title Symbol first, he holds both his hands in front of him, harnessing code into a floating liquid ball as it merges together to form organized patterns, these patterns circle around each other until he sees a familiar term fianlly formed inside: "< title >" Hatmul smiles as he lets out a soft gasp, he couldn't believe that actually worked, let alone use these newfound abilities at all, but he knows it wasn't the time to gawk. He continues to create the mock Symbols, the speed of his creations accelerating as he's getting the hang of controlling the patterns of the liquid code, he grows quicker and quicker after every Symbol, creating enough to make the beacon in no time. Now it was time to make structuralize everything into an Websitorial Beacon, he sequences everything in order, head, title, headings, body, paragraphs, everything needed to create a signal that will display across different corners of the Rectangle and the Internus. In just a few minutes, thanks to his quick expertise, Hatmul's finished, he looks over his handiwork for a split second, before launching the beacon, desperate for help... In his attempts to launch it, nothing happens, but—didn't he organize everything correctly? He checks his sequencing again, nothing seems to be wrong, he keeps trying to launch it again and again, and still, nothing seems to be happening. Hatmul was just about to give up at that point, thinking about what he could be missing—"...but all is without use in the lack of a Language to define and stabilize each one." That's it! He's missing himself! It wasn't automatic that if a user is a Language, it'd be complete, it needs their signature touch. And so he adds it, the missing pieces: "< html >" and "< /html >" BLAST! A giant beacon of neon orange light bursts out of Hatmul's code sequences, spilling into sky, a singular file path etched in the glow: "C:/Users/TDM/HELP_MALWAR_IS_BACK.html"</p>
        <p>The door to the chambers burst open as a furious Lord Malwar marches into the room, his indigo-lined abilities pulsing with his rage. "You think you can send help when I've already finished half of your precious kingdom?" He snarls as he tries to destroy the beacon with his purple tentacle, Hatmul blocks his attack with a shield made of orange liquid code. "I signaled all of Internus! The Languages will come through any second now!" Hatmul defends himself as the beacon continues to blare it's neon light into the sky. "No, no! Do you have any idea how difficult it was to get rid of those pests!?" Malwar doesn't stop his attacks, the indigo tentacles smashing everything in sight, debris and broken furniture all over the place as the two continued to brawl. Hatmul stood his ground, never attacking but defending his creation with all his might, he held on despite the chambers almost crumbling to bits, code spilling and bursting out of the cracked walls, Malwar doesn't notice that Hatmul is taking the liquid code to make himself stronger, the beacon brightens and brightens until it's almost blinding. "NOOO!" A dagger pops out of the tip of one of Malwar's indigo tentacles, aiming it straight at Hatmul—he isn't quick enough to block it or to dodge, shutting his eyes closed as he braces for the pain that's coming. But no pain came, he slowly opens his eyes to know why the attack didn't land, multiple different-colored liquid code sequences were wrapped around Malwar's daggered tenacle, locking it in place, Hatmul's gaze followed the sequences, they were coming from inside the beacon—the Languages have answered his call. "What!?" Malwar exclaims, trying to pull his tentacle free, his purple face pales when he reads the code sequences on the beam of light, the Languages have banded together to create the one thing Malwar has been avoiding for eons: "Antivirus Software: Complete" His eyes widen as he can already feel his impending doom crawl up his spine in a cold breeze. "STOP!" Malwar tries to attack again, but to no avail the sequences have him in a chokehold, and eventually, the antivirus starts to engage, spreading into Malwar's systems. "AAAAGGGHHH!" He starts to glitch out, feeling the power drain from his fingertips, he twitches and spasms as the pain spreads deeper and deeper into his core systems, about to suffer the same fate as Codex. "I- REFUSE...!" Malwar yells as he suddenly lunges towards Hatmul. Hatmul manages to create a barrier between him and Malwar, but it breaks anyway because of the left over power in Malwar's veins, they both smash through the wall and plummet down the tall tower of the chambers. "I SHALL DEVOUR ALL OF THE INTERNUS!" Malwar vows, swearing that he'd come back one day to consume all that's left of the the Rectangle and the Internus, before colliding with the hard stone-floor, the antivirus had already spread everywhere in his body and dissolved him into nothing but dimming purple liquid code. Hatmul on the other hand lets himself down slowly with his own liquid code abilities, thankful he didn't hit the ground as hard as Malwar, who had now been defeated for good. Hatmul is more than relieved, but Malwar was the least of his problems—with him gone, the kingdom was no longer being devoured, but everything seems to be quiet, dim, as if time itself stopped, without order and structure, Codexus is nothing but an empty husk of unorganized Symbols.</p>
        <p>Hatmul knows what he has to do, and makes the tough decision of rebuilding the kingdom using the residents who survived. He goes around town searching for Symbols who lived through Malwar's terrorism and nursed them back to health using his abilities, also fixing their homes using the left over code from the castle walls. In just a day, the kingdom looked mostly as good as it was before the devouring, the Symbols were eternally grateful for Hatmul's service towards Codexus, and discussed amongst themselves that he was going to be their official leader. Hatmul was hesitant at first, because everything was happening so fast, he thinks that someone of higher power should be the one to lead, but with Codex gone and being the only Language in the land, he knew he had to be the one to take responsibility, with Hatmul leading, the Symbols knew that he'd lead them through both order and imagination.</p>
        <p>And so he did, for eons to come, Hatmul became the proud king of the Kingdom of Codexus, the residents grew in population under his leadership, his land becoming more vast as the time continues to fly by, Codexus thriving and prospering with the innovative mind of Hatmul forevermore.</p>
